## Free domains

A free domain needs no <Term tip="The system that points a domain name at a server's address. For your own domain, you add a record at your domain provider.">DNS</Term> setup, so it's the quickest way to get a service online.

It's a <Term tip="A free service that answers any name ending in an IP address with that address.">nip.io</Term> name with your cluster's address in it, like `shop-web.203.0.113.7.nip.io`. Each cluster picks which address its free domains use, under **Free domains** on its **Settings** tab:

- **Public IP:** the cluster's public address. Anyone on the internet can reach it, and it gets a Let's Encrypt certificate.
- **Tailscale:** a server's [Tailscale](/docs/tailscale) address, like `shop-web.100.64.0.2.nip.io`. Only devices on your <Term tip="Your private Tailscale network. Only devices you've added to it can reach addresses on it.">tailnet</Term> can reach it. Pyo Gateway answers there on ports 80 and 443 even while the cluster's [public traffic](/docs/clusters#public-traffic) is off.

By default, a cluster uses **Public IP** when it takes public traffic and a server has its public address itself, on its network card or as its external IP, or a load balancer gives it one. On AWS, Google Cloud, Azure, Alibaba Cloud and Tencent Cloud, a server has only a private address on its card, and the cloud gives it the public one. Pyo reads that address from the cloud and uses **Public IP** once Let's Encrypt reached the server there. See [Opening ports 80 and 443](/docs/install#opening-ports-80-and-443). A server behind <Term tip="A router or provider that shares one public address between machines. Connections from the internet only reach a machine when a port is forwarded to it.">NAT</Term>, like one at home or with some cloud providers, only knows its public address from the internet's side. Ports 80 and 443 may not reach it, so the cluster uses **Tailscale** and the setting says **Behind NAT**. Choose **Public IP** there if ports 80 and 443 are forwarded to the server.

Without Tailscale, a server behind NAT has no free domain. A new service then opens on your own network over HTTP, at the server's address and a port Pyo picks, like `http://192.168.1.20:31234`. Set up [Tailscale](/docs/tailscale) for HTTPS, or forward ports 80 and 443 and add your own domain.

### Tailnet domains

With [Tailscale](/docs/tailscale) set up, a service can also get a name on your tailnet, like `blog.<tailnet>.ts.net`. Only devices on your tailnet can open it, over HTTPS with Tailscale's certificate. It needs no DNS record, since Pyo sets up the name on your tailnet. Choose **Tailscale** when you add a domain. It's there on clusters whose servers are on your tailnet. In the domain list, a tailnet domain shows the Tailscale logo. A name another device or Service on your tailnet already has shows **Taken on your tailnet**, and `pyo` and `registry` are kept for Pyo.

When you change it, the cluster's free domains move to the new address within seconds. A cluster without the chosen address has no free domain. Add your own domain instead.

### In tunnel mode

A cluster with [Cloudflare Tunnel](/docs/cloudflare-tunnel) on gives each service a tunnel domain instead of a free one. Choose **Tunnel domain** when you add a domain. It's a name under the tunnel's default domain, like `shop-web.example.com`. One wildcard record covers them, so they need no record of their own. A record you made for a name comes first. Without a default domain, the cluster has no tunnel domain, and the dialog says to pick one.

Its nip.io domains stop answering from the internet while public ports are off. They answer again when public ports are back on, with nothing to move.

## Custom domains

Add your domain to a service, then point its DNS at your cluster. Pyo checks the DNS and tells you when the domain is active.

Is your DNS on Cloudflare? [Connect Cloudflare](/docs/cloudflare) and Pyo creates the record for you. On a cluster in [tunnel mode](/docs/cloudflare-tunnel), Pyo points the domain at the tunnel instead, and **Switch to Tunnel** and **Switch back** move a domain there and back. In the domain list, a domain that goes through the tunnel, or through a record Cloudflare proxies, shows the Cloudflare logo.

## Changing domains

Adding or removing a domain, or changing its HTTPS settings, takes effect within seconds. You don't need to redeploy, and the service keeps running. A service that hasn't been deployed yet gets its domains with its first deploy.

Domains belong to the service, not to a deployment, so a [rollback](/docs/deploy/rollbacks) keeps the domains the service has now.

## Traffic path

When a cluster has both public ports and Cloudflare Tunnel on, pick the domain's **Path**: **Direct** (ports 80 and 443 of the servers) or **Cloudflare Tunnel**. A new domain is Direct unless public ports are off. Moving a domain drops its open connections once. A domain shows **Not served** while its path is off on its cluster. See [Traffic](/docs/clusters#public-traffic).

## Routes

Several services of a team can share one domain, each on its own path. For example, `example.com` goes to `web`, and `example.com/api` goes to `api`. A request goes to the longest path it's under, so `/api/users` goes to `api`, and `/about` and `/apix` go to `web`.

To add one, open the service's **Domains** page, choose **Add domain**, enter the domain and a **Path**, like `/api`. Turn on **Strip prefix** to send `/api/users` to the service as `/users`.

- The first service to add a domain holds it: its DNS record, certificate, tunnel, redirects and HTTPS settings. Paths on other services follow these. The domain list shows **Shared** on a domain other services also use.
- Each service keeps its own [firewall](/docs/firewall), rate limits and [WAF](/docs/firewall), and its own analytics.
- A path is used once per team. Another team can't use the domain.
- All services on one domain run on the same cluster.
- When the service that holds a domain removes it, or is deleted, the oldest path takes it over with its DNS record and certificate. Pyo offers to delete the DNS record only when the last one goes. Then the domain is gone.
- On Pyo Cloud, you prove you own a domain once. Its paths need no proof of their own.

### Edit routes

The team's **Domains** page lists each domain once. Open a domain to see which path goes to which service under **Routes**.

- **Add a path:** choose **Add path**, enter the **Path** and pick the service.
- **Change a route:** open the route's menu and choose **Edit**. You can change its path, **Strip prefix** and port. For a path, you can also pick another service on the same cluster. The route that holds the domain stays on its service.
- **Remove a route:** open the route's menu and choose **Remove**. Removing the route that holds the domain hands it to another path. Removing the last route removes the domain.

If a move to another service stops halfway, an error says which service each path goes to now. Viewers can see routes but can't change them.

## Redirects

[Pyo Gateway](/docs/gateway) can send visitors from one address to another. Open a domain's menu on the service's **Domains** page and choose **Redirects**. Each redirect has:

- **Path:** the path it applies to, like `/old`. It covers everything under it, like `/old/page`. Leave it as `/` for the whole domain.
- **To:** another hostname, like `example.com`, a path, like `/new`, or both, like `example.com/new`.
- **Status:** 301 for a move that stays, or 302 for one that doesn't.
- **Keep the rest of the path:** on by default, so `/old/page` goes to `/new/page`. Turn it off to send every request to the target itself.

The query string is always kept.

### Redirect a whole domain

To send `www.example.com` to `example.com`, add `www.example.com` with **Only redirect** on, and enter `example.com` under **Redirect to**. Add it to the service that serves `example.com`, or any service of the team. The domain gets its DNS record and certificate like any other, so `https://www.example.com` works too. It has no port.

A domain that also serves a service can't redirect `/` as a whole. Redirect a path instead, or add the domain with **Only redirect**.

### Path redirects

A path redirect goes on the domain itself, on the service that holds it. A path can't be a redirect and a route at once. For example, add `/blog` to `example.com` with **To** set to `blog.example.com` to move a blog to its own domain. More specific paths win, so `/blog` answers before the service at `/`.

Plain HTTP requests are redirected straight to HTTPS when the domain has a certificate. Redirects skip the service's firewall and WAF, since they never reach it. A domain can have up to 15 redirects.

## Headers

Pyo Gateway can set or remove headers on a domain's responses, and on requests to the service. Open a domain's menu and choose **Headers**.

- **Set** adds a header, or replaces it when the service sends one.
- **Remove** takes a header out, like `X-Powered-By`.

Under **Responses**, quick buttons add common security headers: **HSTS** (`Strict-Transport-Security`), **Frame options** (`X-Frame-Options`), **No sniffing** (`X-Content-Type-Options`) and **Hide X-Powered-By**. Under **Requests to the service**, headers go to your service with each request.

Some headers can't be changed, because Pyo Gateway sets or needs them: `Connection`, `Keep-Alive`, `Proxy-*`, `TE`, `Trailer`, `Transfer-Encoding`, `Upgrade`, `Host`, `Content-Length`, `Forwarded`, `Via`, `X-Forwarded-*`, `X-Real-Ip`, `X-Envoy-*`, `Cf-*` and `X-Pyo-*`. Each list takes up to 16 headers.

## In one place

The team's **Domains** page has a row per domain. A green lock before the hostname means HTTPS works. A domain that needs attention shows an icon there instead, and a short note after the hostname, such as **Waiting for DNS** or **Certificate failed**. Hover the lock or the icon for details. A service's **Domains** tab shows the same lock, icons and notes. Each row on the team's page also shows:

- **Routed to:** the service and project the domain goes to, how many services share it, or where it redirects.
- **Requests, 24h:** the domain's requests over the last 24 hours, as a small chart and a total. It shows a dash when the domain had no requests, or when [observability](/docs/observability#turning-it-on-or-off) is off. On a phone, this column is hidden.

Search, filter and sort the list. Open a domain to see its routes, redirects and headers, with its DNS record and certificate.

## From the CLI and MCP

```bash
pyo domain add example.com/api -s api --strip-prefix
pyo domain add www.example.com --redirect-to example.com
pyo domain redirect example.com/old --to /new --status 302
pyo domain redirect example.com/old --remove
pyo domain header example.com --set 'Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000' --remove X-Powered-By
pyo domain header example.com --request --set 'X-Region: eu'
pyo domain header example.com
```

Over [MCP](/docs/mcp), `add_domain` takes `path`, `strip_prefix` and `redirect_to`, and `set_domain_redirects` and `set_domain_headers` replace a domain's rules. Redirects and headers take effect within seconds, without a redeploy.

## Request timeout

[Pyo Gateway](/docs/gateway) waits up to 300 seconds for the service to answer a request on its domains. After that, the visitor gets a 504 error. To change it, set **Request timeout** on the service's **Networking** page, from 0 to 3600 seconds. 0 means no limit. Like a domain change, it takes effect within seconds, without a redeploy.

You can also set it with `deploy.timeout` in [`pyo.jsonc`](/docs/cli/pyo-jsonc), or with the `set_request_timeout` tool over [MCP](/docs/mcp).

The request timeout doesn't close WebSockets. On public domains, a WebSocket or another long stream stays open while it's quiet for up to an hour.

## Certificates

Pyo picks a certificate for you:

- **Public domains** get a <Term tip="A free service that issues the certificates browsers need for HTTPS. Pyo requests and renews them for you.">Let's Encrypt</Term> certificate, issued and renewed automatically.
- **Private domains**, on a <Term tip="Your private Tailscale network. Only devices you've added to it can reach addresses on it.">tailnet</Term> or LAN, get a <Term tip="A certificate Pyo makes itself. The connection is still encrypted, but browsers show a warning because no public authority vouches for it.">self-signed certificate</Term>.

You can also use [your own certificate](/docs/certificates), including wildcards.

### When a certificate isn't issued

While Let's Encrypt works on a certificate, the domain shows **Issuing certificate**. If it fails, or is still pending after 10 minutes, the domain shows **Certificate failed** with the reason. On the team's **Domains** page or the service's **Domains** tab, hover over it to read the reason. Pyo keeps trying on its own.

| Message | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Let's Encrypt is busy for free nip.io addresses this week | Every nip.io user shares one Let's Encrypt limit. On busy weeks it runs out. Add your own domain for HTTPS now. |
| Let's Encrypt's limit for this domain is reached for now | Too many certificates were issued for your domain lately. Pyo tries again later. |
| Let's Encrypt can't reach this server on port 80 | Let's Encrypt checks the domain over port 80. On a home server behind NAT, forward ports 80 and 443 on your router to it. Elsewhere, check that the server's firewall lets port 80 in. |
| The domain doesn't point at this server yet | Its DNS record is missing or points somewhere else. |
| The domain's CAA record doesn't allow Let's Encrypt | Add `letsencrypt.org` to the domain's CAA record. |
| No certificate after 10 minutes | It's been pending too long. The rest of the message says why. |

Requests to your domains go through [Pyo Gateway](/docs/gateway) on each server.

## Other kinds of traffic

Services aren't limited to websites. A port can be:

- **Private:** reachable only inside the cluster. Use this for databases and internal APIs.
- **Website:** an HTTP service on a domain.
- **Port on every server:** a TCP or UDP port on each server in the cluster.
- **Public IP:** a public TCP or UDP port, for a game server or a database you reach from outside.

Only private ports are reachable while the cluster's [public traffic](/docs/clusters#public-traffic) is off. With free domains on [Tailscale](#free-domains), websites stay reachable from your tailnet too.

### Public TCP and UDP ports

A **Public IP** port goes **Through the gateway** by default. The [gateway](/docs/gateway#tcp-and-udp-ports) counts its connections in analytics. For TCP, you can add IP rules, a connection limit and PROXY protocol. The [firewall](/docs/firewall) applies to these ports too.

Turn the switch off to open the port directly on the servers instead. Your service then sees each visitor's address itself. Only servers running one of its replicas answer on the port. The port has no analytics or gateway options. Switching a port between the two interrupts it for about 1 to 3 seconds.

Each cluster can have one public port per number and protocol. Inside the gateway, ports below 1024 are moved up by 10000. For example, 123 and 10123 can't both go through the gateway.

Some ports can't be public, because the servers or Pyo use them:

| Protocol | Reserved ports |
| --- | --- |
| TCP | 22, 53, 80 and 443 (for websites), 2379, 2380, 6443, 10080, 10443, 10250, 19000 to 19003 |
| UDP | 53, 8472, 51820, 51821 |

In [tunnel mode](/docs/cloudflare-tunnel#tcp-and-udp-ports), public TCP and UDP ports stay on the servers by default. They don't go through the tunnel. Turn off **Public TCP and UDP ports** under the cluster's **Traffic** to close them too.
