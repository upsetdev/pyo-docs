---
title: Managing certificates
description: Upload your own TLS certificates, including wildcards.
---

Pyo gets Let's Encrypt certificates for public domains automatically. You only need this page to use your own certificate.

## Upload a certificate

Open **Certificates**, in the sidebar's **Domains** menu, and upload a certificate with its <Term tip="The secret half of a certificate. Whoever holds it can use the certificate, so keep it safe.">private key</Term>. One certificate can cover a single domain, or many with a wildcard like `*.example.com`.

The private key is stored encrypted on the server and is only sent to your team's clusters.

## Use it on a domain

Set a domain's HTTPS to your own certificate, then pick one that covers that hostname.

## Replace or remove

Replace a certificate before it expires, using the same page. Domains using it switch to the new one without a redeploy. Removing one deletes its private key from Pyo.

## Mutual TLS (mTLS)

If a <Term tip="A network of servers, like Cloudflare, that sits between visitors and your servers to speed up and protect your sites.">CDN</Term> sits in front of your services, it can present a certificate to prove that a request came through it. This is called mTLS. [Securing Pyo](/docs/securing-pyo#require-the-cdns-certificate-with-mtls) shows how to require it on your servers.

You can create a certificate when you turn on mTLS in a cluster's **Settings** tab. You can also create one ahead of time from **Mutual TLS**, in the sidebar's **Domains** menu. Give it a name, a <Term tip="The name written into the certificate to identify it. Any label you'll recognize works.">common name</Term> and how long it's valid for. Team owners and admins can do this from the dashboard, but not with an API key.

You get a certificate and a private key to upload to your CDN. Pyo installs the matching <Term tip="The certificate that signed your CDN's certificate. Your clusters use it to check that a certificate is genuine.">CA certificate</Term> on your clusters for you.

<Callout type="warn">
  Save the certificate and private key in a password manager before you close the dialog. Pyo doesn't keep them, so they can't be recovered. Anyone with the private key can pass as your CDN.
</Callout>

If you lose the private key, create a new certificate and replace the old one.

### Replace a certificate

Pyo warns you in the activity feed 30 days and 7 days before a certificate expires. To replace one without downtime:

1. Create a new certificate.
2. In your cluster's **Settings** tab, choose **Manage** under **mTLS** and select both certificates, so your cluster accepts either.
3. Upload the new certificate and key to your CDN.
4. Choose **Manage** again and clear the old certificate.

You can't remove a certificate while a cluster requires it. Removing one only takes it off this page. Your CDN keeps presenting it until you change it there.

### Turn mTLS off without the dashboard

The dashboard isn't behind mTLS, so you can usually turn mTLS off there. If you've put the dashboard on a domain that mTLS blocks, run this on the server:

```bash
sudo k3s kubectl -n kube-system delete tlsoption default
```
