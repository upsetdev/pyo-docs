---
title: Cloudflare Tunnel
description: Take websites in through Cloudflare with no open ports on your servers.
---

With Cloudflare Tunnel, your servers open no ports for websites. A small connector in your cluster dials out to Cloudflare, and Cloudflare sends your visitors through it to [Pyo Gateway](/docs/gateway). Pyo sets up the tunnel, its routes, the DNS records and the certificate for you.

It also lets Pyo run on a server where another program already uses ports 80 and 443, like Traefik or nginx.

## What you need

- Your domains in a Cloudflare account.
- A [Cloudflare token](/docs/cloudflare#create-the-token) with these permissions:

| Type | Resource | Permission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zone | Zone | Read |
| Zone | DNS | Edit |
| Zone | SSL and Certificates | Edit |
| Account | Cloudflare Tunnel | Edit |

- Your servers can reach Cloudflare on TCP port 7844.

After you add the permissions in Cloudflare, choose **Test** on the **Cloudflare** card under **Integrations**. Zones that can use the tunnel show in green. A zone that lacks one of the two tunnel permissions says which one. A zone with neither is **DNS only**.

## Turn it on

1. Open your cluster's **Settings** and find **Traffic**.
2. Pick **Cloudflare Tunnel**.
3. Pick a **default domain** if you want one. Services then get free names under it, like `web-shop.example.com`.
4. Select **Switch**.

Pyo creates the tunnel and starts two connectors, on different servers when it can. Domains in your Cloudflare account move to the tunnel. If a name has a record Pyo didn't make, Pyo asks first. Domains in other accounts, and free nip.io domains, stop answering from the internet.

If the choice is greyed out, the cluster's agent can't run a tunnel yet, or its install is older than Pyo's. Run the command under **Refresh this cluster** in the cluster's settings, then try again.

Team owners and admins change a cluster's traffic.

## Move one domain

On a service's **Domains** tab, select **Switch to Tunnel**. Pyo adds the tunnel's route first, then points the name at the tunnel with a proxied CNAME record. While the record moves, the domain answers both ways, through the tunnel and directly. Once it points at the tunnel, Pyo Gateway only lets the tunnel in. A client that connects to your server directly gets a 403.

If Cloudflare already has a record for the name, Pyo shows it and asks before it replaces it. Pyo keeps a copy, so **Switch back** puts it back. Developers can switch a domain when nothing is in the way. Replacing a record, or putting one back, is for team owners and admins.

When you add a domain on a cluster in tunnel mode, **Through Cloudflare Tunnel** is on in the dialog and in the new service wizard.

## Free domains

With a default domain, a cluster in tunnel mode gives each service a free name under it, like `web-shop.example.com`. Pyo makes one wildcard record, `*.example.com`, that points at the tunnel. Free names need no record of their own. A record you made for a name still comes first, so your existing sites aren't touched.

A name under the default domain that no service has gets a 404.

A name under the default domain always goes through the tunnel, so it has no **Switch back**. That goes for a free name and for a domain you add under it. Pyo refuses to move it back while the default domain covers it. The one exception is a name whose old record Pyo replaced. **Switch back** puts that record back, and it answers before the wildcard.

The default domain must be one of your zones itself, like `example.com`. Cloudflare's free certificate covers one level below a zone, so a name like `web.apps.example.com` wouldn't work.

Without a default domain, a cluster in tunnel mode has no free domains. Only domains you switch go through the tunnel.

## Move sites from Traefik with no downtime

1. Connect the server to Pyo. Pick **Cloudflare Tunnel** for the cluster.
2. Deploy each site as a service in Pyo and add its domain.
3. Select **Switch to Tunnel** on the domain. Traefik keeps serving until Cloudflare points at the tunnel.
4. Check the site. If something's wrong, select **Switch back**.
5. When every site has moved, stop Traefik.

## Visitors

Pyo Gateway takes the visitor's address only from the connectors. Firewall rules, rate limits, the WAF, country rules, analytics and the audit log all see the real visitor. A service doesn't need [Behind Cloudflare](/docs/firewall#behind-cloudflare) for its tunnel domains.

Your service gets the visitor's address in `X-Forwarded-For`, as the only address there. While a domain is still moving, a client that connects directly shows up with its own address, whatever header it sent.

Visitors who type `http://` are sent to HTTPS, whatever your zone's **Always Use HTTPS** says.

## TCP and UDP ports

Cloudflare Tunnel carries websites only. Public TCP and UDP ports stay on your servers' public addresses, or on [Tailscale](/docs/tailscale). They don't go through the tunnel. To close them too, turn off **Public TCP and UDP ports** under **Traffic**.

## Status

The **Traffic** panel shows how the tunnel is doing:

- **Tunnel connected**, with its connectors and Cloudflare's locations.
- **Tunnel degraded**, when a connector is down.
- **Tunnel down**, when no connector reaches Cloudflare. Its websites don't answer. Pyo sends an [alert](/docs/alerts) after 5 minutes.

A connector that can't start says why, like `ImagePullBackOff`.

From the CLI:

```bash
pyo cloudflare tunnel status --cluster eu
```

## Certificate and token

Pyo issues a Cloudflare Origin CA certificate for the tunnel's names. Only cloudflared sees it. Your visitors see Cloudflare's own certificate. It's valid for 15 years, and Pyo issues a new one when a name it doesn't cover goes through the tunnel. It's listed under **Certificates** as **Cloudflare Tunnel**.

The connector runs with the tunnel's own token, kept in your cluster. Pyo never sends your Cloudflare token to the cluster. **Rotate** gives the tunnel a new token and a new certificate.

## Cloudflare's limits

- Uploads over 100 MB fail on the Free and Pro plans.
- Cloudflare waits 100 seconds for a response to start.
- WebSockets work. gRPC needs the zone's gRPC setting in Cloudflare.

Don't change Pyo's tunnel in Cloudflare. Pyo puts its routes back.

## Turn it off

Pick **Public ports** or **Tailscale only** under **Traffic**. Domains keep using the tunnel until you move them back, so both work at once. Then select **Switch back** on each domain, and **Remove tunnel**. Names under the default domain have no **Switch back**. Pyo deletes the tunnel, its certificate and only the records it made, and puts back the records it replaced. Deleting a service or a cluster cleans up its tunnel records the same way.

From the CLI:

```bash
pyo cloudflare tunnel on --cluster eu --default-domain example.com
pyo domain tunnel blog.example.com
pyo domain tunnel blog.example.com --replace
pyo domain tunnel blog.example.com --back
pyo cloudflare tunnel off --cluster eu
pyo cloudflare tunnel remove --cluster eu
pyo cloudflare tunnel rotate --cluster eu
```
